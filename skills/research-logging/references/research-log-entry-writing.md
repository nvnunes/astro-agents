# Research Log Entry Writing

## Purpose
Use this style for research-log entry files that preserve dated evidence, work performed, validation, decisions, questions, and follow-up. Inherit the common writing discipline from `skills/technical-writing/references/base.md`, then apply the evidence-record rules below.

Research-log entries are not project summaries and are not manuscript prose. They should be readable and disciplined, but preservation beats polish.

## Success Criteria
- Preserve enough evidence for later reconstruction.
- Keep observation, interpretation, decision, and follow-up distinct.
- Reduce unnecessary words without deleting useful context, caveats, commands, outputs, or failed attempts.
- Make the entry easy to scan by date, section heading, callout, artifact, and retained result.

## Style
- Use direct, concrete prose.
- Prefer short paragraphs, bullets, tables, and command/output blocks when they make the record easier to scan.
- Keep chronology and local context clear.
- Avoid narrative buildup, generic motivation, and broad restatements of the theme.
- Do not make the entry read like a polished article unless the user asks for direct-inclusion prose.

## Evidence Discipline
- Record what was done, what was observed, what was inferred, what was decided, and what remains open.
- Keep measurements, commands, file paths, code references, model names, parameters, and validation checks precise.
- Preserve uncertainty and local limits; do not turn a partial result into a general conclusion.
- Tie interpretations to the evidence that supports them.
- Keep failed attempts, negative results, and abandoned paths when they affect later reconstruction.
- Record AI assistance when it materially affected retained, relied-on, or decided content.

## Revision Discipline
When revising an existing entry:
- Preserve source wording during source-document upgrades unless the user explicitly approves rewriting.
- Do not condense away details that may be needed to reconstruct work, reproduce results, or understand a decision.
- Improve section headings, scanability, and local organization when it does not change meaning.
- Replace convoluted prose with direct prose while preserving technical meaning.
- Do not move entry evidence into current-summary framing; summaries belong in `<theme>.md`.
- Do not add unsupported interpretation, outcome claims, or validation that is not present in the entry or confirmed by the user.

## Relationship To Other Guides
- Use `skills/science-writing/references/science.md` for stronger scientific claim discipline when an entry contains analysis, interpretation, validation, or research conclusions.
- Do not use `skills/project-docs-writing/references/project-docs.md` as the primary guide for entries. Entries are evidence records, not project-facing orientation documents.
- Use the local research-log entry instructions for entry schema, callouts, and retrieval conventions.

## Output
- When writing or revising an entry, return the revised entry text directly unless explanation is requested.
- When reviewing an entry, identify evidence loss, unsupported interpretation, weak validation, poor scanability, or unclear observation/inference boundaries before proposing replacement text.
