# Semantic Review Catalog

Use this catalog to route researcher-directed semantic Review. Apply groups and
lenses by meaning rather than through a keyword table. Do not start Review on
the agent's own initiative.

## Review Groups

1. **Analysis Review** — Are the methods, implementation, result derivations,
   statistical reasoning, and numerical work sound?
   - Implementation Fidelity
   - Result Derivation
   - Methodological Validity
   - Statistical Validity
   - Numerical Validity
2. **Evidence Review** — Are the sources, evidence, claims, decisions, and
   evidence presentations trustworthy and adequately supported?
   - Source Authority
   - Citation Support
   - Claim Support
   - Claim Calibration
   - Decision Support
   - Evidence Coverage
   - Evidence Selection
   - Presentation Fidelity
3. **Record Review** — Is the research record current, consistent,
   reconstructible, conforming, and clearly written?
   - Summary Fidelity
   - Cross-Entry Consistency
   - Presentation Consistency
   - Workflow Reconstructibility
   - Research-Log Conformance
   - Research-Log Writing

Groups expand to their listed lenses. Keep findings attributed to the
individual lenses and do not assign a group verdict.

## Unqualified Review Menu

Present this menu verbatim for an unqualified request such as “review this
research log”:

1. **Analysis Review** — Are the methods, implementation, result derivations,
   statistical reasoning, and numerical work sound?
2. **Evidence Review** — Are the sources, evidence, claims, decisions, and
   evidence presentations trustworthy and adequately supported?
3. **Record Review** — Is the research record current, consistent,
   reconstructible, conforming, and clearly written?
4. **Choose individual lenses** — Choose from the complete semantic-review lens
   catalog.

Ask which option or options the researcher wants. Present the numbered catalog
below only when the researcher chooses option 4 or when a focused request
remains ambiguous outside the named groups.

## Lens Catalog

Present this list verbatim when individual-lens selection is required:

1. **Source Authority** — Assess whether external data, observations,
   reference data, software sources, or publications are trustworthy,
   authoritative, appropriately versioned, and fit for the stated use.
2. **Citation Support** — Assess whether a cited publication or external
   reference supports the statement attributed to it.
3. **Claim Support** — Assess whether the presented evidence is relevant and
   sufficient for a claim and whether the reasoning warrants the proposition.
4. **Claim Calibration** — Assess whether a claim's certainty, scope,
   generality, and treatment of uncertainty match the strength and limits of
   its support.
5. **Decision Support** — Assess whether a recorded research decision follows
   from the evidence, objectives, criteria, constraints, alternatives, and
   tradeoffs stated by the research account.
6. **Evidence Coverage** — Assess whether the evidence spans the material
   conditions, outcomes, counterexamples, and boundary cases needed to answer
   the research question.
7. **Evidence Selection** — Assess whether choices to include or exclude data,
   cases, runs, configurations, or results are appropriate, transparent, and
   not selectively favorable.
8. **Summary Fidelity** — Assess whether the maintained summary accurately and
   currently represents the entries, including material qualifications,
   conflicts, and superseded results.
9. **Cross-Entry Consistency** — Assess whether entries agree about material
   terminology, units, configurations, assumptions, results, and lifecycle
   state, or identify their differences clearly.
10. **Presentation Fidelity** — Assess whether figures, tables, excerpts,
    captions, and derived statistics communicate their underlying evidence
    faithfully without materially misleading choices or omissions.
11. **Presentation Consistency** — Assess whether figures, tables, and related
    presentation choices use titles, labels, legends, terminology, ordering,
    units, and styling consistently across the reviewed material unless a
    difference serves a clear purpose.
12. **Implementation Fidelity** — Assess whether research code faithfully
    implements the method, configuration, selections, and output construction
    described by the research account.
13. **Result Derivation** — Assess whether a reported result follows correctly
    from its upstream inputs through the substantive calculations and
    transformations in the recorded research workflow.
14. **Methodological Validity** — Assess whether the research method,
    comparison, control, model, and assumptions are suitable for the question
    being answered.
15. **Statistical Validity** — Assess whether sampling, estimation,
    uncertainty, aggregation, and inference support the reported conclusions.
16. **Numerical Validity** — Assess whether numerical methods, approximations,
    convergence, stability, precision, and units make the computed results
    trustworthy.
17. **Workflow Reconstructibility** — Assess whether the complete retained
    research account contains enough information to understand and reconstruct
    the material workflow without executing it or requiring redundant prose.
18. **Research-Log Conformance** — Assess whether the account follows the
    semantic research-logging conventions for section roles, entry boundaries,
    navigation, lifecycle state, and retained-material placement.
19. **Research-Log Writing** — Assess whether sections and the maintained
    summary apply the research-log writing conventions to communicate clearly
    and precisely, organize the account around research questions and outcomes,
    avoid redundant work narration, and form a coherent whole.
