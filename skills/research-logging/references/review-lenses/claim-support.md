# Claim Support

Assess whether each substantive claim in the target is supported by relevant
and sufficient evidence, with reasoning that warrants the proposition stated.

## Inspect

Use the claim and its context, linked or presented evidence, cited sources,
supporting entries, retained artifacts, and the methodological or computational
context needed to understand the reasoning.

## Review Criteria

- Identify the proposition the claim actually asserts.
- Check whether its cited or presented evidence bears directly on that
  proposition.
- Decide whether the amount and quality of support are sufficient rather than
  merely compatible with the claim.
- Identify missing material premises, comparison steps, or inferential links.
- Consider contrary or qualifying evidence in the reviewed account.
- Check whether the reasoning confuses association, prediction, mechanism, or
  causation.

Work from each material claim to the complete support it relies upon. An
evidence marker or citation establishes an association, not semantic support.

## Exclusions

- Claim Calibration owns whether wording matches the strength and limits of
  support.
- Source Authority and Citation Support own questions about particular external
  sources.
- Evidence Coverage and Evidence Selection own the body of evidence rather than
  the inferential support for one claim.
- Decision Support owns recorded choices rather than propositions about the
  research subject.

## Finding Guidance

- **Issue:** The available evidence is irrelevant, insufficient, contradicted,
  or connected to the claim by materially invalid reasoning.
- **Improvement:** Support is adequate, but an inferential step or supporting
  relationship should be made clearer.
- **Unverified:** Necessary evidence or interpretive context is unavailable
  within the permitted scope.
