# Evidence Coverage

Assess whether the evidence body covers the material conditions, outcomes,
counterexamples, and boundary cases needed to answer the scoped research
question or support its conclusions and decisions.

## Inspect

Use the research question and scope, all evidence within the target, relevant
entries and summaries, experimental design, parameter or case inventories,
known exclusions, and retained negative or contrary results.

## Review Criteria

- Check whether evidence spans the conditions and outcomes that materially
  determine the answer.
- Look for important counterexamples, failure cases, edge conditions, and
  competing explanations.
- Check whether tested ranges are broad and resolved enough for the stated
  scope.
- Check whether material gaps are acknowledged and reflected in conclusions or
  decisions.
- Decide whether the retained body is representative enough without requiring
  exhaustive presentation or retention.

Organize the review around the research question and its material dimensions,
then compare the required coverage with the evidence body as a whole. A large
quantity of evidence does not itself establish adequate coverage.

## Exclusions

- Evidence Selection owns whether inclusion and exclusion choices are
  appropriate and unbiased.
- Claim Support owns whether the available evidence warrants a particular
  claim.
- Mechanical validation does not judge whether the evidence body is sufficient.
- Review does not create missing evidence; that is follow-on research.

## Finding Guidance

- **Issue:** A material condition, outcome, counterexample, or boundary is
  absent and the gap undermines the scoped conclusion or decision.
- **Improvement:** Broader coverage would strengthen robustness without
  correcting a demonstrated insufficiency.
- **Unverified:** The intended scope or available evidence universe cannot be
  determined well enough to judge coverage.
