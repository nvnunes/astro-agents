# Methodological Validity

Assess whether the research method—including its design, comparisons, controls,
models, and assumptions—is suitable for the question being answered.

## Inspect

Use the research question, method description, experimental or analytical
design, controls and comparison basis, models and assumptions, relevant domain
references, data characteristics, and retained results needed to evaluate the
method.

## Review Criteria

- Check whether the method answers the stated research question.
- Assess whether comparisons and controls are appropriate and sufficiently
  matched.
- Identify material confounders, dependencies, alternative explanations, and
  boundary conditions.
- Assess the plausibility of model assumptions and treatment of consequential
  limitations.
- Check whether observations, interventions, metrics, and validation strategy
  test what the research claims they test.
- Assess whether the method remains suitable across the scope of the
  conclusions.

Organize the review around the research question and the inferential role of
each methodological choice, not around code structure or work chronology.

## Exclusions

- Implementation Fidelity owns whether code implements the described method.
- Statistical Validity owns sampling, estimation, uncertainty, and inference
  within the design.
- Numerical Validity owns computational reliability.
- Source Authority owns external inputs rather than the method that uses them.
- Review may identify the need for a different method but does not perform or
  choose follow-on research without researcher authorization.

## Finding Guidance

- **Issue:** The design, comparison, control, model, or assumption materially
  fails to answer the question or support the claimed scope.
- **Improvement:** The method is suitable, but an additional check, control,
  qualification, or rationale would strengthen it.
- **Unverified:** The method, assumptions, domain context, or necessary expertise
  cannot be established well enough for judgment.
