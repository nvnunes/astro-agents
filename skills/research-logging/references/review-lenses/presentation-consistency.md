# Presentation Consistency

Assess whether related figures, tables, and presentation surfaces use titles,
labels, legends, terminology, ordering, units, and styling consistently unless
a difference serves a clear purpose.

## Inspect

Use the set of related presentations within the target, their captions and
surrounding prose, and presentation code or configuration when needed to
understand whether a difference is intentional.

## Review Criteria

- Compare names, units, symbols, colors, line styles, ordering, and precision
  for equivalent quantities and categories.
- Compare the level of detail in titles, axis labels, legends, captions, and
  table headings.
- Check whether related presentations retain stable visual conventions across
  sections and entries.
- Determine whether departures from an established convention communicate a
  real distinction or are accidental drift.
- Identify inconsistencies that could make equivalent things appear different
  or different things appear equivalent.

Compare presentations that serve similar communicative roles. Do not derive a
universal style from unrelated figures or tables.

## Exclusions

- Consistency does not require rigid uniformity. Purposeful differences are
  acceptable.
- Presentation Fidelity owns whether an individual presentation faithfully
  communicates its evidence.
- Cross-Entry Consistency owns agreement in research meaning and facts.
- Research-Log Writing owns prose and whole-account communication.

## Finding Guidance

- **Issue:** Unexplained presentation drift materially impairs comparison or
  creates a misleading distinction or equivalence.
- **Improvement:** Harmonization would make the account more coherent or easier
  to compare without correcting a substantive defect.
- **Unverified:** Missing presentations or inaccessible rendering information
  prevents a supported comparison.
