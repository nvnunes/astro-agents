# Source Authority

Assess whether each external source on which the target materially depends is
trustworthy, sufficiently identified, and fit for its particular role. Review
external datasets, observations, publications, reference products, and
software sources together with the roles they serve.

## Inspect

Use the research log, input registry, citations, retained source metadata,
relevant external documentation, publications, release records, and the source
itself when accessible.

## Review Criteria

- Identify the source and relevant version.
- Assess whether its origin and custody are sufficiently trustworthy.
- Assess whether it is suitably authoritative for its assigned role.
- Consider whether its scope, collection conditions, resolution, calibration,
  limitations, and age make it suitable for that role.
- Check whether the account acknowledges limitations that materially affect
  its use.

Organize the review around each distinct external source, not every place where
it appears. Establish its identity, version, authority, and general limitations
once, then assess each distinct role it plays. The same source may be suitable
for one role but unsuitable for another.

## Exclusions

- Mechanical provenance identifies where evidence originates; it does not
  establish authority.
- Citation Support owns whether a reference supports an attributed statement.
- Result Derivation owns whether upstream material was transformed correctly.
- Methodological Validity owns models and simulations. Generated outputs do not
  become external sources merely because they are retained as files.

## Finding Guidance

- **Issue:** A material source is demonstrably unsuitable, unreliable,
  misidentified, or insufficiently characterized for its role.
- **Improvement:** Better identification or qualification would strengthen the
  account without correcting a demonstrated problem.
- **Unverified:** Missing access, identity, lineage, or necessary expertise
  prevents a supported judgment.
